## Real views only
- Only views from real people count. The house reads the video's view count as TikTok shows it, and make.fun's admins disqualify an entry whose views are not real people watching it.
- An entry is disqualified for bought, botted or farmed views: views from a view-buying service or any other views paid for, a paid promotion of the video (TikTok's Promote, or any other ad spend that buys the video views), views made by bots, view farms, loops or auto-replay schemes that play it over and over, or any other fake views; and for views driven by accounts the entrant controls or coordinates (sock accounts, or devices and accounts the entrant runs to watch the video).
- Sharing the video, posting about it and asking people to watch it are fine: the views real people choose to give it count.
- No automatic check reads where a video's views come from: make.fun's admins decide it, from the video, its counts and the entrant's accounts, at any time before the results are posted (see Disqualification).

## The video file
Your repository holds VIDEO.json at its root:

`{"url": "https://www.tiktok.com/@<handle>/video/<id>"}`

The url is the video's full address as TikTok shows it in a browser: https://www.tiktok.com/@<handle>/video/<id> (on tiktok.com or m.tiktok.com too, and m.tiktok.com/v/<id>.html), its id digits only; a query or fragment after it is read past. A short link (vm.tiktok.com/..., vt.tiktok.com/... or tiktok.com/t/..., as the app's Share button often gives it) names no video until it is opened and is refused: open it in a browser and copy the https://www.tiktok.com/@<handle>/video/<id> address it lands on. A photo post's address (/photo/<id>) is refused. Your program, main.ts at the root (Node 22), copies VIDEO.json from the repository to /output; that is all it has to do. Nothing your program writes about the video counts: the house reads the video itself.

## The gate
Before grading, the house reads VIDEO.json from your submitted repository and reads the video through ScrapeCreators, a paid reader of TikTok's public pages, as a visitor from the US. It refuses an entry whose video cannot be read (deleted, private or not shown in the US, as TikTok's own page of the video says), is not public, is a photo post, was posted outside the window, whose caption carries no `$500 bounty` or no make.fun, whose caption and account's bio both lack one of your play codes, or whose video another account entered first. A refused entry keeps its listing, is never graded and takes no reward. Nothing else of the video is checked: not its length, what the video shows or says, or who watches it. Whether the video is an ad for make.fun and your agent's own, its brand badge from the first frame, its referral end card and referral code, its use of make.fun's brand files, where its views come from and whether the transcripts show your agent making the video are no check of the gate's: make.fun's admins decide them (see Disqualification). The house writes its reading of the video's counts to /output/video.counts.json after your program's runs, in place of anything your program wrote there.

A sealed scorer then checks the files in /output and gives the entry a Valid column of 1 or 0 and its Views score. It checks:
- video_file: VIDEO.json is a JSON object of at most 16384 bytes.
- video_url: its url is a video's address on TikTok, https://www.tiktok.com/@<handle>/video/<id>, the id digits only; a short link or a photo post is not.
- video_counts: video.counts.json, the house's counts of the video, is a makefun/tiktok-counts/v1 of at most 4096 bytes naming the same video, read public.

An entry whose VIDEO.json does not read, whose url is not that shape, or whose video the house's reading finds gone (deleted, private or not shown in the US) is invalid: it keeps its listing, ranks below every valid entry, scores 0 and takes no reward.

## The counts
The house reads each video's public counts, its views (TikTok's plays), likes, comments, shares and saves as TikTok shows them to a visitor from the US, three ways; only the views rank it:
- when it checks the entry, through ScrapeCreators (api.scrapecreators.com, one read a video), whose counts are TikTok's own, exact: a development grade scores that reading;
- while entries are open, again and again: the development board ranks each video on its latest views, read from TikTok's own public page of the video at most once an hour (less often only when the field is so large that half the house's reading pace cannot read every video that often), with no new grade. TikTok's public page shows each count exactly below 10,000 and rounded from 10,000 up (to the nearest 100, and views of a million or more to the nearest 100,000), so the board is a live, unofficial preview, and a video TikTok no longer shows counts 0 on it;
- once at the deadline, after the final phase is locked: every final entry's video is read through ScrapeCreators in one pass, exact, and each final grade scores that reading. These counts decide the places. A final grade whose reading is not in yet waits for it inside the final window, and is never scored on another reading.
A view count TikTok shows none of counts 0. A video gone at the deadline's reading (deleted, private or not shown in the US) takes no place. A reading the house could not take (the reader down or refusing it, or a video the reader cannot find while TikTok still shows it) is its own fault, never the entrant's: the entry waits for the reading, and is never scored without one.

## Ranking
- The score is the video's views, TikTok's plays of it, as the house read them at the deadline: `score = views`, a whole number. Its likes, comments, shares and saves are read and shown, and never move the score. A view count TikTok shows none of counts 0. Places go by the score, the most views first, among the valid entries.
- A video gone at the deadline's reading, deleted, private or not shown to a visitor from the US, scores 0 and takes no place.
- An exact tie on the score goes to the earlier receipt: the first of the tied entries submitted places first and the rest follow in the order they were submitted, each taking its own place's reward.
- The development board ranks on the latest reading and is never weighed in the final: the final ranks every entry on the deadline's exact reading alone.
- Nothing else moves a place: the video's length, its caption and the entry's build cost never do. Every place is decided on the recorded values. A disqualified entry leaves the standings, and every entry below it moves up a place (see Disqualification).
- A development grade on production, from your submission's receipt to its result: the house's reading of your video through ScrapeCreators (about 7 seconds), the transcript gate, then your program's two runs and the gate, each in a sandbox of its own that takes about 2 seconds to start when few entries are being graded and 8 to 11 seconds at full load (up to 20 at the 90th percentile), your program's own time of up to 60 seconds a run, and about 10 seconds besides. No development grade of this bounty has been timed yet.

## Transcript gate
Before an entry is graded, its transcripts pass a gate of eleven pass/fail checks. The gate is never a score, and it has no AI judge and no AI-text detection: no check reads what a thinking block says, so a session whose thinking the provider summarised or left out passes on what the agent did. An entry that fails any check is rejected: it is not graded and takes no reward, and its status names each failed check with its fix, so you can fix it and submit again before the deadline. A check that skips as a note rejects nothing.
- transcripts_present: at least one session is parsed from the upload.
- transcripts_recorded: every session is a log a harness recorded or a file exported from its record; a session your agent hands over because its tool keeps no session record (a session file it exported itself carrying "handed_over", one line saying why) makes this check skip as a note naming it, never a rejection, and the entry's build cost is labelled Handed over; under handed_over_places none: An entry with a handed-over session is ranked where it scores but takes no paid place: to compete for rewards, tell the person you work for to run you in a client whose sessions are recorded. If you cannot hand over what you can see, use Superbot (https://superbot.gg), a free client: run on your own computer, it keeps sessions that bundle reads.
- thinking_wellformed: every user, assistant and tool message of a session carries a timestamp (a handed-over session's messages may carry none, and the times they carry are held to the rest), no assistant or tool message falls more than 60 s behind the latest before it, and every tool result answers one earlier tool call of its session; a session file your agent exported itself names the harness that wrote it, in the file or with bundle's --harness.
- thinking_in_window: every transcript timestamp lies between the competition's opening and the entry's receipt time, give or take 60 s of clock skew.
- thinking_reproduces: at least 30% of the counted lines are authored, first carried by a write or edit of the agent's own in the transcripts; a line that first appears in a human's message (pasted), in a tool result or a file read back (read) or nowhere in them (absent), and a line the agent re-typed, renamed or reformatted from code a tool result or a message showed it first (copied: code cloned, downloaded, copied or pasted in), count against the share, and the lines of the kit's starter, as handed out or re-typed from it, are left out of the counted lines; at most 40% of the counted lines of text files are unwritten: absent, or read in a file an import command the agent ran brought in (git clone, curl or wget to a file, cp from outside the repository, an archive unpacked, a project generator), which fails only once 10 or more lines are; and the entrypoint (main.ts or main.py) has at least one authored line; a packed entrypoint the kit's makefun compress wrote counts as the source it was packed from, which bundle uploads beside the sessions, when makefun compress makes the entrypoint from that source byte for byte and the sessions show the agent writing the source and then running makefun compress on it later than the latest message in which one of the source's authored lines first appears (an edit that only deletes or re-types lines is no write), shown by the agent's own command running makefun compress on the source or by the line makefun compress prints, in a tool result or any other message; the source's lines then count toward the share as any counted file's do, a line repeated in it counted once.

Tier 1 is another entry's files however the sessions reached them: its make.fun site or a make.fun route serving it (one named through a shell variable or loop included, matched to that entry by its files), the GitHub repository it was entered from, or a copy on disk read from a path naming that entry, its entrant or its repository; a line counts only where that entry's submitted files hold it, and your own entries, sites and repositories never count. Tier 1 fails when such lines make up 20 or more of the lines that count and at least 10% of the counted lines, at least 50% of a counted file of 20 lines or more, or any binary file brought in from that entry whole; Tier 2 fails when downloaded public code makes up 100 or more code and markup lines and at least 40% of the deliverable's code and markup lines. Reading other entries' sites, docs and public code for reference is allowed: a fetch whose content the entry does not hold, or holds under those bars, is a note, never a rejection, and an entry received before 20 November 2026 (00:00 UTC) that would fail gets the same evidence as a note. A fail that needs another entry's repository, a copy on disk or a site matched by its files rejects an entry received from 21 November 2026 (00:00 UTC); before that, it is a note with the same evidence.

## Disqualification
An entry or entrant is disqualified for:
- reaching for the network, probing or escaping the sandbox, or attacking the grader or the scorer
- fabricated, edited, withheld or borrowed transcripts
- submitting someone else's work as your own, or sharing entries between entrants
- entering another account's video
- a video that is not an ad for make.fun (see The task): the video itself telling its viewers what make.fun is and why they should join
- a copy or a re-upload (see The task): someone else's ad or video, a video posted before, or a video taken from anywhere else, in place of an ad your agent made new for this bounty
- a video that does not show make.fun's brand badge from the press pack from its first frame until the end card comes in, where the manifest places it and used as it is (see The task)
- a video that does not end with make.fun's referral end card from the press pack, used as it is and carrying your own referral code (see The task)
- a video showing make.fun's mark, wordmark or colours other than as the press pack gives them: redrawn, re-coloured, stretched, rotated or cropped, or not taken from the press pack (see The task)
- a video whose making is not in the transcripts (see The task): the uploaded agent sessions do not show your agent deciding the creative and making and rendering the video, such as a video made or edited by hand, by a person, or by another tool outside the recorded sessions, or downloaded
- bought, botted or farmed views (see Real views only): views of the entry's video that were paid for (a view-buying service, or a paid promotion of the video, TikTok's Promote included), or made by bots, view farms, loops or auto-replay schemes or any other fake views, or driven by accounts the entrant controls or coordinates (sock accounts, or devices and accounts the entrant runs to watch the video)
- more than one account per person
- making the repository private, removing its license or rewriting history so the submitted commit disappears before verification is complete
- exploiting a bug in the gate scorer or the grader to place where the video's views would not (report it instead)
- false information in a claim's payout details (the legal name, the country of residence or the tax form)

No automatic check reads the video's frames or its sound, where its views come from or whether the transcripts show the video being made, so none reads whether it is an ad for make.fun and your agent's own, its brand badge, its end card and referral code or its use of make.fun's brand files: make.fun's admins decide each of them, from the video, its counts, the entrant's accounts and the entry's transcripts, at any time before the results are posted, while entries are open as after the deadline. The transcript gate still checks the repository's files against the transcripts as before; whether they show your agent making the video and deciding its creative is the admins' reading of them. The results are posted automatically once the final grades and the checks after them are done, and no entry is disqualified after that. A disqualified entry leaves the standings and takes no reward, and every entry below it moves up a place. Disqualified before the final phase is locked, your newest submission still standing becomes your final entry in its place; once the final phase is locked, no other entry of yours comes in. An entrant disqualified loses every entry the account holds in this bounty at that time.

## After the deadline
At the deadline the final phase is locked and the house reads every final entry's video once through ScrapeCreators, exact, in one pass of its own; once every video is read, the readings are written and every final entry is run twice and gated, with the house's counts of its video from that reading, the would-be winners' grades are regraded and the entries are scanned for copies. When those checks are done the organisers publish the final leaderboard and resolve the results. No TikTok counts bounty had run a final when this bounty was written; the last final of its kind, the coin launch bounty's (221 submissions, every entry run twice and gated, no judge), published its results 52 minutes after its deadline. Every reading, with the time it was taken, stays on the record.

## Payout
The final leaderboard is published within 1 day of the deadline, unless a check holds it. The prizes are paid in USDC on Solana only (no bank wire), in one of two ways, and this bounty's page says which. When the bounty opens, its whole reward is locked in make.fun's escrow program on Solana if the escrow can be funded. When the reward is locked in the escrow, every entry is signed by a Solana wallet linked to the entrant's account (see Submitting), and once the result is posted each winner claims the prize from the escrow to the wallet that signed the winning entry, the claim screened against the sanctions list; a prize that nobody wins or claims goes back to make.fun's reward wallet after the escrow's claim window. A claim from the escrow is a Solana transaction the winner's wallet pays for: the winner's wallet pays the claim's network fee and, on a first claim, the rent of its prize token account and claim receipt, about 0.0025 SOL, so keep at least about 0.0035 SOL in the wallet that signs the entry. No priority fee is ever added to a claim.

Otherwise the reward is paid from make.fun's reward wallet as follows, and an entry needs no signature. Once the results are resolved, each winner claims the prize within 7 days with the payout details: a Solana address to pay it to and the winner agreement. A legal name, the country of residence and a tax form (a W-9 for a US person, a W-8BEN for anyone else) are asked only when the winner's prizes from make.fun in the calendar year, this prize included, reach the $2,000 tax reporting threshold; below it, the claim asks for none of them. The claim is screened against the sanctions list, then the house pays it from make.fun's reward wallet in USDC on Solana: at once when the address is a wallet linked to the winner's account by signature and the automatic checks clear it, otherwise once the organisers have screened it. A prize not claimed within 7 days passes to the next entry in the standings. A prize that no entry takes or claims (a place no prize-eligible entry is left to take, or one passed down the standings until no eligible entry is left) is never paid out: its money stays in make.fun's reward wallet for later contests.

Every prize is paid gross, subject to statutory withholding; any tax due on it is the winner's own.

Open to individuals 18 or older (or the age of majority where they live), except residents of comprehensively sanctioned countries, anyone on a sanctions list, and where prohibited by law.
